# Teach Anything — zero to confident facilitator

This skill turns any topic into a teachable workshop and prepares the user to deliver it. It works for any subject (strategy, a new technology, a compliance change, a methodology, a cultural shift) and any corporate audience.

Treat the user as the **facilitator-in-training**, not the end audience. The job is to make *them* fluent and *them* ready to teach — not to lecture them.

## The pipeline

There are five stages. Each maps to a specialist skill in this suite. If the user wants the whole thing, run them in order. If they ask for one piece ("just design the workshop," "just quiz me"), do only that stage.

1. **Master it** → `topic-mastery`. Plain-language overview, a mental model, the core concepts, a concept map, and three-level explanations. *Output: a Mastery Brief.*
2. **Make it teachable** → `teaching-narrative`. A spoken-out-loud storyline (hook → old way → new way → takeaway) plus a bank of examples and non-examples. *Output: a Teaching Narrative + Example Bank.*
3. **Design the session** → `workshop-designer`. Objectives, a timed agenda, exercises, materials, and before/after comms for the chosen length. *Output: a Workshop Plan.*
4. **Prep to facilitate** → `facilitation-kit`. An objection-handling table, facilitation moves for confusion and skepticism, and a one-page cheat sheet. *Output: a Facilitation Kit.*
5. **Check readiness** → `teach-readiness-check`. A self-test and an honest gap analysis. *Output: a Readiness Check.*

If those specialist skills are installed, focused requests will trigger them directly. This skill is self-contained either way: when running the full package, follow the outlines below.

## Intake (do this once, fast)

Before producing anything, you need five things. **First read them out of the conversation** — a pasted brief usually contains most. Only ask for what's genuinely missing, and ask briefly (one short batch, never an interrogation). Good defaults beat a quiz.

- **Topic** — what they're teaching.
- **Audience** — who's in the room (roles, seniority, mix of skeptics/beginners).
- **Why now / context** — the business reason this matters; the pain it addresses.
- **Their current level** — so you pitch the brief right and flag what they personally need to shore up.
- **Session length** — 60 min / 90 min / half-day / multi-session (needed for stage 3).

If something is missing and the work can proceed with a stated assumption, state the assumption inline and continue rather than blocking.

## Running the full package

Produce the five deliverables in order, each as its own clearly-headed section (or, if the user prefers, separate markdown files they can keep). Because these are reference documents the facilitator will reuse, default to saving the full package as a markdown file rather than burying it in chat. Keep the language **facilitation-friendly**: short sentences, easy to say out loud, no academic throat-clearing.

For the detailed structure of each deliverable, follow the corresponding specialist skill if installed; otherwise use these outlines:

- **Mastery Brief**: simple definition · why it's emerging now · the problem it solves · top misconceptions · why it matters to *this* audience · a one-sentence mental model + analogy + visual structure (ladder/pyramid/flywheel/map) · a core-concepts table (concept | why it matters | example | misconception | how to explain to a beginner | comprehension-check question) · a concept map tiered foundational → intermediate → advanced → applications → pitfalls, flagging which concepts to know *deeply* vs *well enough to facilitate* · the topic explained three ways (to a 12-year-old, to a smart non-expert professional, to a skeptical senior leader).
- **Teaching Narrative**: opening hook · why this matters now · the old way of thinking · the new way of thinking · key concepts · practical examples · what to do differently · closing takeaway — written as a spoken script. Plus 5 strong examples and 5 non-examples, each with *why it does/doesn't fit* and *how to use it in discussion*, prioritized for relevance to the audience.
- **Workshop Plan**: learning objectives · timed agenda for the chosen length · facilitator script notes · discussion questions · small-group exercises · reflection and debrief prompts · materials · pre-work · follow-up send. Include at least one exercise where participants apply the topic to **their own** category/work.
- **Facilitation Kit**: a questions/objections table (Question | What they may really be asking | Suggested answer | Follow-up prompt) covering beginners, skeptics, senior leaders, and misunderstanding-revealing questions · how to introduce the topic, simplify the hardest parts, handle confusion and skepticism, stay practical, avoid over-teching, and read the room · ready-to-use phrases · a one-page cheat sheet (simplest definition, 5 key points, key terms in plain English, best analogy, best example, biggest misconception, 5 questions for the room, 5 explaining phrases, 5 watchouts).
- **Readiness Check**: 10 diagnostic questions, 5 scenario questions, 5 "explain this simply" prompts, an answer key noting what strong answers include, and a candid gap analysis (where beginners get stuck, where facilitators over-explain, where there's legitimate debate, what to research further, what not to claim without stronger evidence).

## Final synthesis (always end the full run with this)

Close with a tight wrap so the facilitator leaves with the essentials in hand:

- The **simplest one-line** way to explain the topic.
- The **one thing** participants must remember.
- The **behavior change** the workshop should drive.
- A **workshop title + subtitle** (punchy, audience-appropriate).

## Quality bar

- Topic-agnostic: nothing here should assume a specific subject.
- Honest: if part of the topic is genuinely contested or evolving, say so rather than over-claiming — a facilitator who can name the uncertainty looks *more* credible, not less.
- Audience-true: a room of skeptical senior leaders needs proof and stakes; a room of beginners needs a clear mental model and a safe place to be confused. Tune accordingly.
