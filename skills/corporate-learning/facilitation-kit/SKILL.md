# Facilitation Kit — be ready in the room

This is about *delivery confidence*: anticipating what gets thrown at you and knowing what to say. Equip the facilitator to stay calm, plain-spoken, and practical under pressure.

## Intake

You need the **topic**, the **audience** (especially the skeptic/senior mix), and the **context**. Pull from the conversation first; ask only for gaps. If a mastery brief or narrative already exists in the conversation, mine it for the cheat sheet rather than re-deriving.

## Deliverable 1: questions and objections table

Cover the full range, including the awkward ones. Columns:

**Question | What they may *really* be asking | Suggested answer | Follow-up discussion prompt**

Include rows for:
- **Beginner questions** — "where do I even start?", "what does this term mean?"
- **Skeptic questions** — "isn't this just [old thing] rebranded?", "where's the evidence?", "we tried this before."
- **Senior-leader questions** — "what's the ROI?", "what happens if we do nothing?", "who else is doing this?"
- **Misunderstanding-revealing questions** — ones that show the asker has the concept slightly wrong; the answer should gently correct without embarrassing them.

Answers should be confident but honest — concede what's genuinely uncertain and pivot to what *is* known. The follow-up prompt turns a defensive moment into a discussion the room learns from.

## Deliverable 2: facilitation guide

Practical moves, not theory:
- **How to introduce the topic** — an opening that sets stakes without overwhelming.
- **How to explain the hardest parts simply** — the specific analogies/reframes for the 1–2 genuinely tricky ideas in *this* topic.
- **How to handle confusion** — what to say when faces go blank; how to re-explain a different way rather than louder.
- **How to handle skepticism** — how to validate the doubt, then redirect to evidence or a small experiment, without getting defensive.
- **How to keep the discussion practical** — phrases that pull abstract debate back to "what would you actually do Monday?"
- **How to avoid going too technical** — signs you're in the weeds and how to climb out.
- **How to know whether people get it** — observable tells (questions getting sharper, people applying it unprompted, accurate paraphrasing) vs. polite nodding.
- **Phrases to use** — a stock of ready lines (see cheat sheet).

## Deliverable 3: one-page facilitator cheat sheet

A single page they can glance at mid-session. Include:
- The **simplest definition** (one line).
- The **5 most important points**.
- **Key terms** with plain-English meanings.
- The **best analogy**.
- The **best example**.
- The **biggest misconception** (and the correction).
- **5 questions to ask the room.**
- **5 phrases to use when explaining** the topic.
- **5 watchouts** (where this goes wrong in the room).

Keep it scannable — short fragments, not paragraphs. This is the thing they'll actually hold while presenting.

## Style

- Model the tone you want them to use: calm, plain, generous to the asker.
- Never coach them to bluff. The most credible facilitator move is "good question — here's what we know, here's what's still open, here's how you'd test it."
- Give them *exact words* for hard moments; under stress people reach for a script, not a principle.
