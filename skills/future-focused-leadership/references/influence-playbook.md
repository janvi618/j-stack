# Influence Playbook

How to help the user pitch future-oriented work to skeptical stakeholders. Future-focused leaders usually fail not because their analysis is wrong but because their **framing** is wrong. Never lead with abstraction; lead with changed economics, altered risk, or faster decisions.

## Translation rules

Apply these automatically when drafting anything stakeholder-facing:

| Futurist language | Business language |
|---|---|
| Long-term risk | Future **cost of inaction** |
| Weak signal | Early indicator affecting an **existing KPI** |
| Scenario | **Decision trigger** |
| Innovation | **Option value plus learning** |
| Resilience | Margin protection, service continuity, speed |

## Stakeholder framing table

| Stakeholder | What they care about | Framing that works | A good ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFO | Capital discipline, downside risk, hurdle rates | "I'm not asking you to fund a prediction. I'm asking for a staged option with explicit stop/go triggers and a clear downside cap." | A ring-fenced small-bets budget and trigger-based reviews |
| Sales / commercial leader | Volume, customer relationships, channel efficiency | "This signal matters because customer economics and buying behavior are shifting before our plan assumptions do." | One customer- or channel-specific pilot with a clean measurement plan |
| Brand / marketing leader | Penetration, differentiation, relevance | "This isn't abstract; it changes who our next buyer is, what they value, and what our brand must prove." | One sharpened audience hypothesis and one product/claim test |
| R&D / product leader | Pipeline quality, speed to market, claims resilience | "We don't need ten moonshots. We need three targeted experiments that improve our option set if this future starts arriving." | A focused experiment slate tied to scenario assumptions |
| Operations / supply-chain leader | Service, cost, availability, resilience | "This is a resilience and uptime issue before it becomes a strategy headline." | Dual-source, flexibility, or inventory-policy pilot on top-risk inputs |
| CEO | Strategic coherence, investor credibility, enterprise priorities | "This is a now-to-next choice: how do we protect the core while earning the right to win in the next curve?" | Sponsorship for a cross-functional future-forces review and quarterly foresight memo |

Adapt the rows to the user's actual org (e.g., CTO, head of people, board) using the same pattern: identify what they're accountable for, frame the future issue as protecting or improving that, make the ask small/specific/reversible.

## Conversation scripts

Offer these as starting points the user can adapt:

**CFO:** "Three things are true at once: the signal is still uncertain, the downside of doing nothing is rising, and we don't need a heroic investment to learn. I want approval for a staged option: small pilot now, formal trigger review in ninety days, expansion only if we see movement in revenue quality, margin, or risk reduction."

**Sales leader:** "I'm not trying to distract the team with trends. I'm trying to prevent us from defending last year's plan assumptions in a market that's already changing. Let's test one specific hypothesis and keep the scope narrow."

**Brand/marketing leader:** "The risk isn't that the future arrives tomorrow. The risk is that customer logic changes before our brief does. Let's pressure-test the brand against three futures."

**R&D/product leader:** "We don't need a broad brainstorm. We need a small portfolio of experiments that make us smarter about the areas where technology, regulation, or customer behavior could move fast."

**Operations leader:** "I see this as an availability and margin issue, not a foresight exercise. Which two inputs or nodes would hurt us disproportionately if conditions changed quickly?"

**CEO:** "This is about strategic timing. The question isn't whether the future force is real philosophically. It's whether we want to be forced into a reactive move later or buy ourselves a low-cost right to move earlier."

## Meeting tactics

- Bring **three scenarios, not fifteen trends**
- Tie every signal to an existing KPI: revenue, gross margin, inventory, service, risk, or talent velocity
- Use watch points and trigger points, never generic "we should monitor this"
- Make the ask **small, specific, and reversible**
- Invite one provocateur and one operator so the work is both challenged and grounded
- Senior leaders need to know what an insight means **today, next year, and a decade from now** — answer all three horizons in one frame

## Reputation management

How to become known internally without becoming "the trend person":

- Always bring **signal + implication + action**, never signal alone
- Show fluency in both future language and P&L language
- Keep artifacts short and decision-oriented
- Make operators feel helped, not judged
- Be willing to say: "This signal may be noise — but here is the low-cost way to learn"

The litmus test of credibility: do finance, sales, R&D, and operations say your foresight improved **execution**?
