# The Corporate Team Operating System

A simple operating system that imports elite-team lessons into business. These templates are the practical core of the skill. When a user needs structure, give them the relevant template *filled in for their situation* — don't just hand over a blank form. The power of these is consistency, not cleverness.

Contents:
- Team charter
- Roles & decision-rights (RAPID-style)
- Meeting cadence
- Meeting norms
- Conflict norms
- Retrospective
- Feedback loop
- Trust-and-accountability rituals

---

## Team charter

A charter helps *only if it is linked to real trade-offs, decision rights, and review routines.* A laminated values poster is not a charter. Revisit it rather than treating it as a one-time exercise.

- **Purpose.** Why does this team exist, and what enterprise outcome would get worse if it disappeared?
- **Primary outcomes.** What are the two or three business outcomes this team owns *this quarter*?
- **Trade-offs.** What will this team optimize for when speed, cost, quality, customer impact, and internal alignment conflict?
- **Customers.** Who are the team's internal and external customers?
- **Ways of working.** What do we expect from one another when work gets hard?

> Coaching note: the trade-offs line is the one most teams skip and the one that matters most. Force a real answer — "when speed and quality conflict on launches, we hold quality and move the date" — not a both/and dodge.

## Roles and decision-rights

For every major topic, define:

- **Owner** — accountable for movement and outcome.
- **Decision-maker** — makes the call after input.
- **Core inputs** — people who *must* be consulted before the decision.
- **Execution leads** — accountable for implementation.
- **Escalation trigger** — the condition that forces the issue upward.

**The rule that does the work:** *If everyone is accountable, no one is accountable.* When a user describes diffuse ownership or stalled decisions, this template is almost always the fix. Push them to name a *single* decision-maker per topic; "we decide together" is usually how nothing gets decided.

## Meeting cadence

- **Daily / ad hoc huddle** — 10–15 min. Blockers, risks, resource shifts, urgent decisions *only.*
- **Weekly operating review** — progress against outcomes, commitments due this week, decisions needed, red flags, cross-functional dependencies.
- **Monthly learning review** — what changed in the market, customer, system, or internal capability? What assumptions no longer hold?
- **Quarterly reset** — reconfirm mission, priorities, roles, and *what the team will stop doing.*

> Coaching note: when a team complains about meeting overload, the fix is usually not "fewer meetings" in the abstract — it's matching each meeting to a distinct purpose and killing the ones that broadcast information that could have been written. Remote/hybrid teams especially: meetings are for ambiguity reduction, conflict resolution, or genuine synthesis — not status broadcast.

---

## Meeting norms

- Agenda and desired decisions go out in advance.
- Every item is labeled **update**, **discussion**, or **decision.**
- The owner starts with a **recommendation**, not a data dump.
- Challenges target **assumptions and implications, not motives.**
- Every decision ends with **owner, deadline, and communication plan.**
- **No hallway vetoes** after the meeting.

---

## Conflict norms

The goal is not to suppress conflict — it's to keep rigorous disagreement *about the work.* Relationship conflict is destructive; task disagreement is useful when trust and safety keep it focused.

- Disagree **early.**
- Name the **issue, not the person.**
- Use **evidence** where possible.
- If you disagree but can live with the call, **commit publicly** (disagree-and-commit).
- If you believe the decision creates **material risk, escalate explicitly** — don't hint politely.
- Do **not relitigate** a decision unless assumptions changed.

## Retrospective

Use five questions:

1. What were we trying to achieve?
2. What actually happened?
3. What helped performance?
4. What hurt performance?
5. What will we change next time, **by whom, and by when**?

> The fifth question is the whole point. A retro without an owner and a date is performative reflection. Keep it blameless — focus on contributing causes, assume good intent and the information people had at the time.

## Feedback loop

- **Weekly pulse** on three things: clarity, blockers, and risk.
- **Monthly review** of recurring friction points and missed commitments.
- **Post-project review** within one week of major launches or failures.
- **Quarterly team-health review** using the diagnostic (see `diagnostic.md`).

---

## Rituals that build trust and accountability

The most useful rituals are lightweight and repeatable:

- A **kickoff pre-brief** before major work.
- A **quick post-brief** after key customer moments.
- **Regular recognition of invisible support behaviors** (the backup work that usually goes unseen).
- A **standing review of open commitments.**

The point is not ceremony. The point is predictable coordination.

---

## How to deploy these (sequencing)

Don't dump all eight templates on a user at once. Sequence by failure mode:

- **Diffuse ownership / stalled decisions** → roles & decision-rights first.
- **Fuzzy priorities / activity-not-progress** → charter (esp. the trade-offs line).
- **Useless or overloaded meetings** → meeting cadence + meeting norms.
- **Same mistakes recurring** → retrospective + feedback loop.
- **Avoidance or grandstanding** → conflict norms + the leader's response patterns in `leadership-moves.md`.

A full turnaround sequence (which templates to install in what order over 90 days) lives in `leadership-moves.md`.
