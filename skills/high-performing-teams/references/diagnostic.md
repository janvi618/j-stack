# Team Health Diagnostic and Warning Signs

Use this when a user wants to *assess* a team — either a quick read on what's wrong, or a repeatable score to track over time. Offer to run it with them; the interpretation matters more than the number.

---

## Warning signs of a low-performing team

The earliest signs are **behavioral, not financial** — they show up long before the results do. Watch for:

- Diffuse ownership ("everyone and no one owns it")
- Recurring surprise (the same kind of thing keeps blindsiding the team)
- Excessive meeting load
- Conflict avoidance
- Hidden side conversations (the real decisions happen in the hallway)
- Repeated missed commitments
- Information hoarding
- Slow decisions on *known* issues
- Performative agreement (everyone nods, nothing changes)
- Toxic positivity around obviously bad plans

Each maps to one of the underlying failure modes: weak clarity, weak safety, weak communication, weak learning. When a user describes their team, listen for these and name the pattern.

---

## The high-performing team diagnostic

Rate each statement from **1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).**

1. We can state our team's top priorities in the same way.
2. We know who owns each important decision.
3. People raise risks early without being punished socially.
4. We debate ideas honestly without making it personal.
5. Information moves quickly to the people who need it.
6. Our meetings end with clear commitments and owners.
7. We run useful retrospectives and change behavior after them.
8. We know where expertise sits across the team.
9. We trust one another to deliver.
10. Our team can adapt quickly when assumptions change.

### Mapping items to conditions

| Items | Condition | Fix lives in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear direction & priorities | charter → `operating-system.md` |
| 2, 8 | Roles, decision rights, transactive memory | decision-rights template → `operating-system.md` |
| 5, 6 | Communication quality | meeting norms → `operating-system.md` |
| 3, 4 | Candor, safety, trust | leader response patterns + conflict norms → `leadership-moves.md`, `operating-system.md` |
| 7, 10 | Learning velocity & adaptability | retro + feedback loop → `operating-system.md` |
| 9 | Trust & delivery | reduce overcommitment, enforce follow-through → `leadership-moves.md` |

### Interpretation (where to start)

- **Low on clarity items (1, 2)** → fix goals and decision rights *first.* Everything else is harder without this.
- **Low on safety/conflict items (3, 4)** → fix leader response patterns and norms — start with how the leader reacts to bad news.
- **Low on learning items (7, 10)** → install review rituals and action tracking.
- **Low on trust/delivery items (9, and 6)** → reduce overcommitment and enforce follow-through; trust is often a *delivery* problem, not a *feelings* problem.

> Coaching note: don't average the ten into one score and call it a day — the *shape* is the signal. A team scoring 4s on trust but 2s on clarity needs something completely different from a team with the reverse. Find the lowest cluster and start there.

---

## Running it well

- Have each team member score independently, then compare — the *spread* on an item (some 5s, some 2s) is often more revealing than the average. Big spread on "we know who owns each decision" means people have genuinely different mental models of who's in charge.
- Re-run quarterly to track whether interventions are working (this is the quarterly team-health review in the feedback loop).
- Pair it with the two open-ended turnaround questions from `leadership-moves.md`: *where does work get stuck,* and *where does bad news go to die.*
