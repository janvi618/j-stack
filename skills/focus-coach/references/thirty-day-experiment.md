# The 30-Day Focus Experiment

For someone who wants a concrete program to follow rather than ad hoc tips. It's a four-week arc that moves from *observing* to *redesigning* to *practicing* to *integrating*. Run it as a coach: introduce one week at a time, check in on what actually happened, and adjust. Don't dump all four weeks at once as a wall of instructions.

The framing that makes this work: **the first week is diagnosis, not performance.** The most common way people sabotage this is by treating week 1 as a test they're failing — logging their distractions and feeling ashamed. Head that off explicitly. They're a researcher studying their own attention, not a defendant.

## Week 1 — Baseline and attention audit
**Goal:** See the truth of how attention is actually spent, without judgment.

**Daily practice:** Log work in ~30-minute chunks. Note interruptions, phone pickups, energy, mood, and one "flow" rating (how absorbed did the best stretch feel?).

**Track:** deep work blocks, interruptions, context switches, on-screen switches, energy, mood.

**Most common failure:** judgment and shame instead of observation. **Adjustment:** treat the baseline as a diagnosis, not a performance review. The data is information, not a verdict.

## Week 2 — Environment and distraction redesign
**Goal:** Change the *conditions*, since most distraction is structural.

**Daily practice:** Remove the phone during one block, batch email to twice daily, clear visual clutter, add calendar buffers.

**Track:** phone pickups, number of inbox checks, meeting spillover.

**Most common failure:** trying to redesign everything at once. **Adjustment:** change only 2-3 high-friction cues. A few changes that stick beat a dozen that don't.

## Week 3 — Deep work and flow practice
**Goal:** Build the core habit of protected, demanding concentration.

**Daily practice:** One protected 60-90 minute block daily on a clear, high-challenge task.

**Track:** minutes of uninterrupted focus, subjective flow, quality of output.

**Most common failure:** choosing vague or impossible tasks. **Adjustment:** predefine the goal and the first step before starting. (Vague target → nothing for attention to lock onto. Impossible target → anxiety, not flow. The challenge has to be hard enough to require full involvement but not so hard that breakdown is likely.)

## Week 4 — Integration into work and life
**Goal:** Extend high-quality attention beyond work, where the meaning payoff is largest.

**Daily practice:** Keep the work block; add one *non-work* absorption block — reading, exercise, a hobby, or relationship presence.

**Track:** meaning rating, recovery quality, leisure quality, weekly quality of work.

**Most common failure:** turning the whole system into self-punishment. **Adjustment:** keep it sustainable and "dailyish," not perfect.

## Coaching notes

- **Check in between weeks.** Ask what actually happened, not what they intended. The adjustments above are the real content — most learning comes from hitting a failure mode and correcting.
- **Let the metrics serve self-knowledge, not scorekeeping.** The end-state isn't a perfect streak; it's a person who understands their own attention well enough to design for it.
- **It's fine to slow down.** If week 2 hasn't landed, don't rush to week 3. The point is durable change, not finishing on schedule.
- **The destination:** not a rigid optimized machine, but someone who protects a few windows for demanding and meaningful activity, batches the rest, recovers properly, and evaluates days by whether attention was used well — not just by how much got produced.
